Tutorial & Help

Importing word lists from Google Sheets or Excel

In Google Sheets or Excel, put the pair in column A and the word in column B. Select the cells and copy them, then paste into the Backup box in Settings and press Import. You can also download the sheet as a CSV (File, Download, Comma Separated Values), open it in a text editor, and paste that.

pair,word
BX,Box
HR,Hair
LQ,Liquid

Tab-separated pastes straight from a sheet also work, and the header row is optional. Pairs use letters A to X. Importing adds to your existing words and overwrites any matching pairs.

Speffz grid spreadsheets work too. Put the first letters (A to X) down the first column and the second letters across the top row, with the word in each crossing cell. Row G and column N gives the pair GN. Labels like words or X / SH are cleaned up automatically (X / SH counts as X), and empty cells are skipped. Every imported word is converted to lowercase.

,A,B,C
A,aqua,abbey,acorn
B,bat,bubble,
C,cat,cabin,cucumber

JSON is accepted too:

{"words":{"BX":"Box","HR":"Hair"}}

Where words appear

Saved words automatically appear directly below the timer and pair as soon as you stop the timer. In batch mode, each pair’s word shows in order.

Keyboard shortcuts

  • [Space] Start or stop the timer; after stopping, press it again to load the next pair
  • [\] Undo the most recent stopped time and mark that pair as failed
  • [Esc] or [R] Reset: cancel the current run
  • [Tab] Edit Word after stopping, then [Enter] to save
  • [C] Memo chain: memorise a longer chain of pairs, then recall it
  • [D] Lookup: find or edit any pair’s word
  • [Y U I O P J H K L N M B], punctuation keys, or [8 9 0]: mark an active attempt as failed without logging a time

Randomisation modes

  • 3BLD IRL (True Random): draws randomly from your active letter-pair pool. The same pair never appears twice in a row.
  • Slow / Failed / Untested Review: prioritises pairs with no recorded single-pair time, yellow or red recall times, or a failed attempt. Untested pairs stay here until tested. A green result removes a pair from this review pool; yellow, red, and failed pairs remain available for more practice. Green pairs are still available in True Random.

Heatmap that follows improvement

The heatmap uses a recent-weighted average rather than treating every old attempt equally. Each new single-pair time shifts the colour toward your current speed, so a pair can genuinely move from red to green as you improve. Your complete history remains available for the session and leaderboard, but it does not permanently hold the heatmap back.

Heatmap averages and time-lapse

The outlined cells on the right and bottom edges are the average for each first letter (row) and each second letter (column), with the overall average in the corner. They show whether a whole letter is the problem rather than single pairs. Below the grid, drag the slider or press play to watch your heatmap change day by day; Live returns to today. Failed marks only show in the live view.

Drill these

In the stats drawer, Drill these starts a session on exactly your slowest pairs from the last 48 hours. Tap the Drilling chip under the timer to end it and see a recap of how each pair compares with before. Drill attempts count in your stats and heatmap like normal.

Drill a letter from the heatmap

Click any row letter (first letter) or column letter (second letter) on the heatmap to start drilling every pair that starts or ends with it straight away. Tap a row or column average cell to get a Drill button instead. The Drilling chip under the timer ends it and shows a recap.

Memo chains

Press [C] or the Ch button. Memorise 4 to 10 pairs in order, press Space, then type them back. Results show which positions you got right, with your saved words. Memo chains are stored separately and never change your heatmap, times, streak or stats.

Failed pairs on the heatmap

A pair you mark as failed turns darkest red with a × on the heatmap, so it stands out from merely slow pairs. It returns to its normal colour once two clean single-pair recalls clear it from the missed-word list. Hover or tap the cell to see how many times it has been failed.

Lookup and leaderboard

Press [D] or the Aa button to look up or edit any pair’s word. The stats drawer shows your 10 slowest single pairs from the last 48 hours, with their words. The clock at the top left is your session time.

Multi-pair attempts

When Pairs per attempt is 2 or more, the final time keeps your accent colour and the attempt is left out of the heatmap and the fast/medium/slow counts, since those thresholds are for single pairs.
